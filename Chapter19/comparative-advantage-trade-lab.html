Comparative Advantage: Who Should Make What?

Explore three different examples. Calculate opportunity costs, decide which country should specialize, and experiment with the terms of trade. This lab uses simplified, fictional production numbers—not actual national production statistics.

Your goal: Explain why absolute advantage is different from comparative advantage, and when trade allows countries to consume beyond what they can produce alone. Allow about 15–20 minutes.

1. Calculate opportunity cost

To find the cost of producing one unit of Good 1, divide the maximum output of Good 2 by the maximum output of Good 1.

Formula: Opportunity cost of 1 Good 1 = Maximum Good 2 ÷ Maximum Good 1
