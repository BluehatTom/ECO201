See how the simulation works

This is a teaching game, not an economic forecasting model. Each policy has preset directional effects: tariffs typically raise domestic prices and help competing producers but reduce some imports; retaliation can reduce exports. Quotas limit quantity without automatically collecting tariff revenue. Trade deals lower barriers but can increase competition for protected firms. Subsidies can support production but cost taxpayers. Diversification may improve resilience but has up-front costs.

For transparency: effects are expressed as game-index points, not empirical percentages. The partner's response depends on the policy, the game's relationship score, and whether a conflict already exists. Changes accumulate over six turns. The balanced score combines outcomes rather than representing any official economic statistic.
