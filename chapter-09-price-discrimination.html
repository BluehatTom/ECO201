How third-degree price discrimination works

  1. One-price monopoly chooses a single price across both demand groups.
  2. With verified separation between groups, the venue can choose MR = MC in each group and charge different prices.
  3. Demand elasticity shapes the price: the group with less elastic demand at its optimal price generally pays more.
  4. Higher venue profit need not mean higher total consumer surplus. Some buyers may gain while others lose.

The model assumes fully separated buyer segments and no resale. It abstracts from capacity constraints, identification costs, fairness considerations, and legal restrictions.
