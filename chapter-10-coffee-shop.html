Why does entry erode profit?

  1. Product differentiation gives the firm a downward-sloping demand curve, so it has some pricing power.
  2. In the short run, it chooses quantity where MR = MC, then sets the corresponding demand-curve price.
  3. Positive economic profits attract entry. Close substitutes cause each existing firm's demand to shift left.
  4. In a stylized long-run equilibrium, typical economic profit approaches zero; firms may still price above marginal cost and operate with excess capacity.

Illustrative model: entry/exit is one firm per year, driven by a representative profit-maximizing firm. Your chosen price determines your own profit but does not independently determine entry.
