1. Why did demand move?

Cloud firms and other buyers wanted more GPUs and specialized AI accelerators at every given price. That shifts the whole demand curve right.

2. Why didn't supply keep up?

Leading-edge fabrication, high-bandwidth memory, and advanced packaging require investment, equipment, and time. Supply responds with a lag.

3. Why does the price rise?

Through 2026 the modeled demand shift exceeds the supply response, raising both the price per AI chip and the number of chips sold. This is a scenario, not a claim that every chip became more expensive.

Real-world price check: Epoch AI’s semiconductor price research puts A100 accelerators around $10k–$15k in 2022–23, H100s around $27k–$35k in 2023, and GB200 systems at approximately $33k–$42k per GPU in later generations. These are different products, not like-for-like inflation: Epoch estimates that the same H100/H200 product group actually became less expensive from 2023 to 2025. The graph shows economic price pressure for a changing category of high-end AI accelerators, not a literal market transaction series.

Scope and sources: Historical shift explanations draw on NVIDIA, TSMC, and Epoch AI’s shipment research. The October 2026 assessment uses TSMC’s October 15, 2026 earnings materials.
